What this answers

How the whole business stacks up — revenue → COGS → gross profit → opex → financing, by fiscal year.

How to read it

ACL has no SKU-level COGS (numCogs is null on every sales row), so margin is business-unit level from the GL journal. GP% is the key metric; financing (LC interest) is the largest recurring drag. GL posting only starts FY2025-26 — earlier years show revenue but no GL.

Basis: Revenue = Sales Local (GL 3010001, net of returns). COGS = GL 4810001 (+ manufacturing 4010001 when >5% of revenue — the date-packing cost). GP% = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue. Other income (GL 3010004) shown separately.

What this answers

How strongly volume responds to price — and why it can't be measured cleanly here.

What β means

β is own-price elasticity. For ACL it is not measurable: units are mixed (MT / carton / pack / kg) and the fruit line's collapse is a supply/procurement halt, not a price response.

Directional read — the fruit collapse

Read with care. Volume collapsed ~99% while price was mixed — consistent with a procurement halt (no stock to sell), not price-driven demand destruction. Treat this as a supply-side signal, not a demand curve.

Method

  • Revenue = numNetValue (orders), ৳ Cr. Deficit revenue uses the GL journal (net of returns).
  • Segment = keyword classifier on item category/subcategory (4 lines).
  • Realised price = revenue ÷ quantity (per SKU, native units — no single blended unit).
  • GP% = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue, GL-level. Manufacturing added to COGS when >5% of revenue.
  • Fiscal year = July–June; label = start year (FY2026-27 = "2026").
  • GL map: revenue 3010001 · COGS 4810001 · manufacturing 4010001 · selling 4310001 · logistics 4410001 · marketing 4210001 · admin 4110001 · financial 4510001 · depreciation 4610001 · other income 3010004.
  • No SKU-level COGS — per-SKU margin is not published; the price-realisation screen is used instead.
