What this answers

How the whole business stacks up — revenue → COGS → gross profit → opex → financing, by fiscal year.

How to read it

AITL has no reliable SKU-level COGS, so margin is business-unit level from the GL journal. GP% is thin but positive; financing (LC interest) is what turns a small gross profit into a deep net loss. GL posting only starts FY2025-26 — earlier years show order revenue but no P&L.

Basis: Revenue = Sales Local (GL 3010001, net of returns). COGS = GL 4810001. GP% = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue. Financing = GL 4510001 (LC interest / bank charges on imported IT inventory). Other income (GL 3010004) shown separately.

What this answers

How strongly volume responds to price — and why it can't be measured cleanly here.

What β means

β is own-price elasticity. For AITL it is not cleanly measurable: units are mixed (units / cable drums / licenses) and sales are project-lumpy (large orders land unpredictably), so price-volume pairs are noise, not a demand curve.

Directional read — the Personal Systems collapse

Read with care. Personal Systems volume collapsed ~90% while prices were mixed — consistent with fewer project wins and a shrinking deal pipeline, not price-driven demand destruction. Treat this as a demand/pipeline signal, not an elasticity curve.

Method

  • Revenue = numNetValue (orders), ৳ Cr. Deficit revenue uses the GL journal (net of returns).

  • Realised price = revenue ÷ quantity (per SKU, native units — no single blended unit).
  • GP% = (revenue − COGS) ÷ revenue, GL-level.
  • Fiscal year = July–June; label = start year (FY2026-27 = "2026").
  • GL map: revenue 3010001 · COGS 4810001 · selling 4310001 · logistics 4410001 · marketing 4210001 · admin 4110001 · financial 4510001 · depreciation 4610001 · tax 4710001 · other income 3010004.
  • No reliable SKU-level COGS — per-SKU margin is not published; the price-realisation screen is used instead.
